What we keep, and who can see it

You can read every page here without an account and without telling us anything. This page is about what happens when you make an account or send us a note.

What an account keeps

  • Your email address.
  • The name you chose to show with your posts.
  • What you posted.
  • What you liked, unliked and reported.
  • Whether you ticked "Email me news from Liberty Politics", and when.
  • If you told us you make videos: that, and the link to your channel.

We never see your password. It is kept scrambled, in a form nobody here can read.

Where it is kept

In Firebase, a service run by Google, in an account that only the people who run this site can open.

Who can see what

  • Everyone: what you posted, the name you chose, and how many likes and unlikes a post has.
  • Nobody but you and the people who run this site: your email address, what you liked, unliked and reported, and whether you ticked the news box.

Other readers cannot see who liked a post. The author of a post cannot see who reported it.

We do not sell any of this, and we do not pass it on to anyone.

Emails from us

One email when you make an account with a password, to check that the address is yours. One if you ask to reset your password. Nothing else, unless you ask for news.

Making an account does not put you on a mailing list. Ticking "Email me news from Liberty Politics" does. The box starts empty, and we keep the date and time you ticked it. News is one email a week at most.

To stop the news, untick it: tap your name at the top of any page, clear the box, and save. You are off the list from that moment.

The list is never shared with any other site.

What you send through the box

What you send through the box at the bottom of a page is a post. It shows on the page it is about, under the name you chose. That goes for a correction, a bug or an idea as much as for an opinion or a video, and for an answer to another reader.

A correction, a bug, something we missed or an idea also goes to a form that the people who run this site read, so that somebody acts on it. That copy carries the page you sent it from, your name, your email address and the number of your account, so that we can tell a reader from a machine. When you report a post, the reason you picked goes to the same form without your name or your email.

What stays on your own device

A note you have written and not sent yet, so that it is still there after you make an account. And the fact that you are signed in, until you sign out.

Other places these pages load from

The pages are served by GitHub, which like any host sees the address your device connects from. The lettering comes from Google Fonts. A video that a reader shared shows its picture from YouTube; the video itself loads from YouTube, X or Instagram only after you tap it.

There is no advertising here, and nothing follows you from page to page.

The one thing we count

We give out two short addresses on the show: LibertyPolitics.com/join and LibertyPolitics.com/panel. Each time one is used, it adds one to that day's total, along with which of our own links was followed (the one under a video, for example). It is a number in the same Firebase account. It holds nothing about who you are, where you are or what device you use.

Deleting it

Tap your name at the top of any page, then "Delete my account". That removes what you posted, your likes, unlikes and reports, and the account itself, there and then.

The copies of corrections, bugs and ideas in the form are kept apart. Ask in the discussion about this site and we delete those too.

Reaching us

Post it in the discussion about this site and pick "Correction".

Liberty Score grades voting records. It does not endorse or oppose any candidate for office, and it is not connected to any party, campaign or committee. Portraits of members of Congress are official congressional photographs. Photographs of other candidates are from Wikimedia Commons, cropped, and each is credited on the photo credits page. Vote data: Voteview, the Clerk of the House, the Secretary of the Senate and Congress.gov. Candidate lists: the state election offices, as compiled on Wikipedia, checked against the Federal Election Commission. Politicians outside the United States are graded on what they have said and done, each line with its source, not on votes.
