    What we mean by liberty

    Most scorecards ask one question: how small does this politician want the government to be? We ask two.

    1. Does this member defend a free society at home: free speech, free markets, a secure border and no religious law?
    2. Does this member stand with people fighting for freedom abroad, or leave them to the regimes that jail and kill them?

    Low taxes at home do not make up for votes that protect the Islamic Republic. A member who is strong on one question and weak on the other gets a middling grade here. That is on purpose.

    12 measures, 232 recorded votes and bills. A member is scored on a measure only where there is a real vote or a real signature to count.

    Ends the Islamic Republic
    Maximum pressure on the regime in Tehran: sanctions, no deal that lets it survive, and no limits on America's power to act against it.
    Stands with the Iranian people
    Backs the people of Iran against their rulers: human rights sanctions, an open internet, and support for those in the streets.
    Stands with Israel
    Treats Israel as an ally, backs its right to defend itself, and votes against cutting off its weapons.
    Stands by America's allies
    Backs Ukraine against Putin and Taiwan against Beijing. Rejects isolationism, on the left and on the right.
    A strong military
    Funds and equips the armed forces of the United States.
    Stops mass migration
    Secures the border, enforces the law, and deports those who break it.
    Limits migration from Islamic countries
    Backs travel limits and strict vetting for entry from Islamic countries.
    Stops the Islamization of the West
    Votes against the Muslim Brotherhood, against supporters of Hamas, and against religious rule in the West.
    Keeps religion out of law
    No religious law in government, and the same freedom for believers and non-believers.
    Free markets
    Lower taxes, less regulation, no price controls, and no to socialism.
    Free speech
    Against censorship, by government or at its request.
    Against the woke left
    Against race and gender ideology in schools, sports, the military and the federal government.

    How a grade is made

    1. The recordWe start from the official roll calls of the House and the Senate. Speeches, posts and interviews count for nothing.
    2. The sideFor every vote we count, we say which side is the liberty side, and why. The list is public, and it is the same list for every member.
    3. The letterEach measure gets a score out of 100. The grade is the plain average of the measures, and every measure weighs the same.
