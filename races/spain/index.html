      What happens next

      1. Nov 13The campaign opens; it closes at midnight on 27 November. Source
      2. Nov 29Spain votes. Source
      3. Dec 23The new Congress and Senate meet for the first time, at 10am. Source
      4. ThenThe King meets every party and proposes a candidate. The first vote needs 176; if it fails, a second vote 48 hours later needs only more yes than no votes. Source
      5. ThenIf no candidate wins within two months of the first vote, the King dissolves the Cortes and a new election is called. Source

      What Spain votes on next

      • Nov 292026

        General election (Congress of Deputies and Senate)

        Called early: the royal decree dissolving parliament was published in the official gazette on 6 October 2026. Without it the vote was due by August 2027.

        All 350 seats in the Congress of Deputies, which then votes in the prime minister, and 209 elected seats in the Senate.

        Voters choose a party list in one of 52 constituencies, the 50 provinces plus Ceuta and Melilla. Seats in each constituency are shared out in proportion by the D'Hondt method among lists with at least 3% of the vote there. The new Congress meets on 23 December 2026 and votes on a prime minister: a candidate needs an absolute majority of 176 on the first vote, or more votes for than against on a second.

        Source: Público / EFE, El BOE publica el decreto que disuelve las Cortes y convoca las elecciones del 29NSource: Público / EFE, El BOE publica el decreto que disuelve las Cortes y convoca las elecciones del 29N
      • May 232027

        Municipal elections, with regional elections in ten regions

        The electoral law fixes the day as the fourth Sunday of May; the formal decree calling the vote has not been issued yet.

        Every town council in Spain and, on the same day, the regional parliaments of ten of the seventeen regions and the assemblies of Ceuta and Melilla.

        All town councils are renewed on the same day across the country. Voters in Asturias, the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands, Cantabria, Castilla-La Mancha, the Valencian Community, Madrid, Murcia, Navarre and La Rioja also elect their regional parliament. Andalusia, Catalonia, Galicia, the Basque Country and Castile and León vote on their own calendars.

        Source: Diari de Tarragona, Calendario electoral: Pedro Sánchez y la incógnita de un posible adelanto
